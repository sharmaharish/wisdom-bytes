Name: Hedonic Adaptation
Category: cognitive-biases
Tags: happiness, psychology, expectations, satisfaction

The tendency of humans to return to a relatively stable baseline level of happiness regardless of major positive or negative life changes. Winning the lottery or getting a promotion provides only a temporary emotional boost before adaptation sets in. This "hedonic treadmill" explains why material gains rarely produce lasting fulfillment and why people constantly chase new sources of satisfaction. Countering hedonic adaptation requires deliberate practices like gratitude, variety, and investing in experiences over possessions.

Example: Someone who buys a dream car feels euphoria for the first few weeks, but within months the thrill fades and they begin eyeing a newer model, returning to their previous happiness baseline.
