Name: Decision Matrix
Category: decision-frameworks
Tags: decision-making, analysis, evaluation

A Decision Matrix (also known as Pugh Matrix or weighted scoring) evaluates multiple options against a set of weighted criteria. Each option is scored per criterion, multiplied by the criterion's weight, and summed. It forces explicit quantification of trade-offs and reduces cognitive biases like recency or anchoring. It's most useful when the decision involves multiple stakeholders with different priorities.

Example: A team choosing a database for a new service lists options (PostgreSQL, MongoDB, DynamoDB) and criteria (consistency — weight 5, scalability — weight 4, operational familiarity — weight 3, cost — weight 2). Scoring each option per criterion, multiplying by weights, and summing gives an objective ranking that depersonalizes the debate.
