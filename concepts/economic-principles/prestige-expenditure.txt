Name: Prestige Expenditure
Category: economic-principles
Tags: signaling, status, economics, consumption

An anthropological concept describing how individuals and groups use material possessions to signal social status and compete for standing within a hierarchy. These expenditures — luxury cars, designer clothing, lavish parties, fine art — function less as consumption and more as communication. The value lies not in the item's utility but in what it says about the owner's place in the social order. Prestige expenditure shapes social norms and drives demand for exclusive goods and experiences.

Example: A company founder hosts an elaborate annual gala at a private venue, not because they enjoy parties, but because the visible expense signals to peers, employees, and investors that the business is thriving and they are a person of influence.
