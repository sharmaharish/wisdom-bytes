Name: Tesler's Law (Conservation of Complexity)
Category: engineering-laws
Tags: complexity, design, architecture, trade-offs

Every application has an inherent amount of irreducible complexity that can only be shifted, not eliminated. Good design often requires that developers absorb most of the complexity through smart defaults and algorithms so that the user's interaction is simpler. If your UI requires the user to manage many settings or steps, you've conserved complexity in the wrong place.

Example: Scheduling meetings is inherently complex. A tool like Calendly hides that complexity from the user by handling it internally, whereas an email chain to schedule a meeting places that complexity on the user. In both cases the complexity still exists — it's just been shifted to a different party.
