Name: Givers, Takers, and Matchers
Category: mental-models
Tags: reciprocity, collaboration, organizational-behavior, success

Adam Grant's framework divides workplace interactions into three reciprocity styles. Givers help others without expecting immediate return, takers aim to get more than they give, and matchers operate on tit-for-tat, keeping score. Contrary to intuition, givers tend to be the most successful long-term—they build strong networks, trust, and collaboration. However, givers risk burnout if they lack boundaries; strategic giving to fellow givers and using tit-for-tat as a safeguard prevents exploitation.

Example: A colleague who freely shares credit, helps juniors learn, and answers questions without angling for favors is a giver. Over time, that person builds a reputation that attracts opportunities and loyal collaborators, while takers get short-term gains but alienate their network.
