Name: Institutional vs. Personal Religion
Category: mental-models
Tags: spirituality, structure, experience, meaning

William James distinguished between institutional religion—the churches, doctrines, rituals, and hierarchies that create communal structure and continuity—and personal religion, the direct, inward, transformative experience of the divine. Institutional religion provides shared symbols and social order but can become rigid and dogmatic. Personal religion is subjective, mystical, and resists codification yet fuels genuine spiritual insight and renewal. The two are interdependent: institutions sustain and contextualize personal experiences, while personal experiences keep institutions vibrant and responsive.

Example: The Protestant Reformation was a shift from institutional authority (the Catholic Church's hierarchy and doctrines) toward personal religion—direct relationship with God, individual interpretation of scripture, and inner conviction over external ritual.
