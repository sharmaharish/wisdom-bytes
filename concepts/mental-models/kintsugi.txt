Name: Kintsugi
Category: mental-models
Tags: repair, imperfection, resilience, beauty

A Japanese art form ("golden joinery") of repairing broken pottery with lacquer mixed with gold, silver, or platinum. Rather than hiding cracks, kintsugi highlights them, making the repair a celebrated part of the object's history. Originating in the 15th century, it embodies the philosophy that breakage and repair can create something more beautiful and valuable than the original. Applied to life, kintsugi reframes failure, trauma, and mistakes not as things to hide but as sources of strength and character that make us unique.

Example: A family heirloom vase shatters. Instead of throwing it away or gluing it invisibly, you have it repaired with gold-dusted lacquer so the golden seams become the most striking feature—turning an accident into a masterpiece and honoring the object's full story.
