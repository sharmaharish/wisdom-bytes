Name: Wabi-sabi
Category: mental-models
Tags: imperfection, aesthetics, acceptance, design

A Japanese aesthetic and philosophy centered on finding beauty in imperfection, impermanence, and incompleteness. Wabi-sabi values simplicity, natural aging, and the quiet elegance of worn or weathered objects. It is closely tied to the tea ceremony, where a crackled, handmade bowl is appreciated more than a flawless, mass-produced one. As a life philosophy, wabi-sabi teaches contentment with what we have, acceptance of life's transience, and joy in the present moment rather than constant striving.

Example: A ceramic teapot with an uneven glaze and a visible hairline crack is considered more beautiful and valuable than a factory-perfect teapot, because its irregularities tell a story of craftsmanship, use, and time.
