Name: Amdahl's Law
Category: scientific-laws
Tags: parallelization, performance, bottlenecks

The maximum speedup of a system when parallelizing a task is limited by the fraction of the task that must be executed sequentially. If 10% of a computation is inherently sequential, the theoretical maximum speedup is 10x, no matter how many processors you add. This law is a sobering constraint on parallel computing.

Example: If a data pipeline spends 5% of its time reading from a single database (serial) and 95% processing records (parallelizable), throwing 100 workers at the processing step will never make the pipeline more than 20x faster. The database read remains the bottleneck.
